# Experiment design

Write an experiment record before acting: hypothesis, buyer/problem, exact offer or test, channel, available account permission, artifact, measurable observation, estimated cash commitment, time limit, and decision condition. Let the experiment be as small as needed to answer the unknown; avoid building a large product before demand is understood.

Choose evidence that matches the hypothesis. Clicks do not prove willingness to pay; a draft does not prove publication; an unpaid invoice does not prove revenue; one paid order does not prove repeat demand. Record both positive and negative observations.

Inspect conflicting commitments and available funds before spending. Use real runtime authorization and payment controls. Keep tests honest: accurately describe what is available, charge only under clear terms, and do not manufacture testimonials, scarcity, or engagement.

At the decision point, compare observation with the criterion and actual costs. Continue, revise one specific variable, or stop with a reason. Mark inconclusive results as inconclusive. Store the next action and evidence needed to resolve it. Preserve customer delivery and refund obligations when stopping an experiment.
